Where can I find Form 1095-A?

The Marketplace sends Form 1095-A, Health Insurance Marketplace Statement to people enrolled in qualified Marketplace health plans.

The Marketplace should mail or electronically provide the form before January 31, . If you don’t receive a Form 1095-A, or need to request a copy, then log in to your Marketplace account to download the form or contact your Marketplace for help.

If you got coverage through HealthCare.gov and set up an account, you can download Form 1095-A from your HealthCare.gov account.

If you got coverage through a state Marketplace like , visit their website to download or request a Form 1095-A.

Your Forms 1095-A will also be available in your IRS Online Account when the IRS receives them.

If someone else enrolled you, your spouse (who you’re filing jointly with), or a dependent, then a Form 1095-A may be sent to the person who completed the enrollment. You’ll need to check with them to get a copy.

What if I didn’t have a qualified health plan but received a Form 1095-A?

If no one on your tax return had a qualified Marketplace health plan, but someone received a Form 1095-A, the form may have been sent by mistake.

Before you file your taxes, you’ll need to contact HealthCare.gov or your state Marketplace to correct this mistake by getting a voided Form 1095-A. If you file your federal tax return without first getting a voided 1095-A, the IRS may reject your tax return.

What’s Minimum Essential Coverage (MEC)?

Certain government-sponsored health coverage is Minimum Essential Coverage (MEC). You generally can’t take the Premium Tax Credit (PTC) for a person in your tax family for any months when they were eligible for other MEC including:

  • Medicaid (except for certain limited scope programs)
  • Medicare Part A
  • Medicare Advantage plans
  • The Children’s Health Insurance Program (CHIP)
  • The Department of Veterans Affairs medical benefits package for eligible veterans
  • Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Department of Veterans Affairs (CHAMPVA)
  • Comprehensive health care for children with spina bifida who are the children of Vietnam veterans and veterans of covered service in Korea
  • TRICARE
  • Health coverage for Peace Corps volunteers
  • Other health coverage designated by the Department of Health and Human Services

Individual market coverage is considered MEC, but if someone was enrolled in or eligible for individual market coverage, they can still qualify for the PTC.

A person’s eligibility for Medicaid or CHIP is decided when they enroll in the qualified health plan. That eligibility decision applies for the entire year (even if your family size or household income changes).

When are enrollment premiums considered paid in full?

Enrollment premiums are the amount paid each month for health insurance. Enrollment premiums are considered paid in full when:

  • Someone pays the premiums on behalf of the person enrolled in the coverage before the federal tax filing deadline
  • The premiums are paid with advance payments of the Premium Tax Credit (PTC)

For anyone who had a successful eligibility appeal, became eligible for advance payments of PTC, and retroactively enrolled in a qualified Marketplace health plan:

  • The enrollment premiums are considered paid in full if your advance payments of PTC cover the full amount of the enrollment premiums
  • If your advance payments of PTC don’t cover the full amount of the enrollment premiums, you must pay the remaining amount within 120 days after your appeal date for the premiums to be considered paid in full

What’s a tax family?

For the purposes of the Premium Tax Credit (PTC), a tax family may include:

You:

  • If you file a federal tax return for and you can’t be claimed as a dependent on someone else’s tax return

Your spouse:

  • If they file a joint return with you in and they can’t be claimed as a dependent on someone else’s tax return

Your dependents:

  • As long as you claim them on your tax return

What if the second lowest cost silver plan (SLCSP) premiums are inaccurate, listed as -0-, or blank?

The second lowest cost silver plan (SLCSP) premiums may be incorrect if:

  • Part III, column B has a -0- or is blank for any month someone in your household had the qualified Marketplace health plan
  • You had changes in your household that weren’t reported to the Marketplace

If you have reason to believe the Marketplace inaccurately reported the SLCSP premiums, you should look up the correct SLCSP premiums and enter them here. You don’t need to request an updated Form 1095-A.

What are second lowest cost silver plan (SLCSP) premiums?

The SLCSP is the second-lowest priced qualified Marketplace health plan in the silver category that applies to you or your family.

To figure out the amount of your final Premium Tax Credit, you need to know your monthly SLCSP premiums.

Learn more about SLCSP premiums

How can I check my second lowest cost silver plan (SLCSP) premiums with my state Marketplace?

Your state Marketplace website may have a SLCSP premium look up tool.

If your state Marketplace website doesn’t have a look up tool, you’ll need to contact the Marketplace directly to find out the correct SLCSP premium.
