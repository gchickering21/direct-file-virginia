What’s a W-2?

A W-2 shows how much money an employee earned and the amount of taxes withheld from their paycheck throughout the year.

Depending on the employer, W-2 forms may look different, but they should all have the name "Form W-2 Wage and Tax Statement" on the form.

See an example W-2

Who gets a W-2?

Employees should receive their W-2 from their employer by January 31 of each year. Employers may mail a paper copy or share an electronic version.

Missing W-2s

If you lost or never received your W-2, ask your employer for a copy. If you still haven’t received the missing form(s) by the end of February, call the IRS at for help.

What employee income isn’t yet supported in Direct File?

In Direct File, you can report everything that appears on a W-2 in except:

  • Allocated tips (box 8)
  • Nonqualified plans (box 11)
  • Some of the codes in boxes 12 and 14. We’ll let you know if this situation applies to you
  • Certain payments received from the state for caring for someone living in your home that are excluded from gross income
  • Amounts received for services performed while an inmate in a penal institution

You also can’t report any income from a job that doesn’t appear on a W-2. For example:

  • Tips not reported on a W-2 (unreported tips)
  • Household employee wages not reported on a W-2

Reporting these types of income requires filing additional forms and schedules that Direct File doesn’t currently support.

If you received money from any of these sources, you’ll need to file using another tool. Find other ways to file your federal taxes.

Why can’t I change my SSN?

You gave the IRS your SSN when you used your ID.me account to sign in to Direct File.

ID.me collected this information from you when you created your account.

You can’t change your SSN in Direct File unless you change your SSN in ID.me first. This helps prevent identity theft.

If you need to change your SSN, contact ID.me, then try signing in to Direct File again.

What if the Social Security number on the Form W-2 is incorrect?

Can an ITIN be used instead of a SSN?

No. An ITIN cannot be used in place of a SSN for work purposes. There must be a Social Security number listed on the W-2.

An individual with an ITIN who later becomes eligible to work in the United States must obtain an SSN.

Incorrect last name

Incorrect first name or middle initial

What if the address on the W-2 is outdated?

If the address is outdated, let the employer know. They’ll need to update their records, but they won’t need to issue a new W-2.

Why can’t I change the country?

If you must use an address outside the U.S., you’ll need to file using another product. Learn more about your filing options.

Why are we skipping box d?

A control number is an internal number used by your employer. We don’t need it to calculate your taxes.

What are federal income tax withholdings?

Your withholding is the amount of federal income tax withheld from your paycheck and other sources. If you’re an employee, your employer probably withholds income tax and pays it to the IRS in your name. Tax may also be withheld from certain other income — including pensions, bonuses, commissions, and gambling winnings.

If too little is withheld, you’ll generally owe tax when you file your tax return and may owe a penalty. If too much is withheld, you’ll generally be due a refund.

If you’re self-employed or don’t pay your taxes through withholding, you may need to pay estimated tax directly to the IRS as quarterly payments.

What are dependent care benefits?

Box 10 shows the total value of dependent care benefits provided by an employer. These benefits are used to care for dependents, such as young children and family members with disabilities.

What are nonqualified plans?

A nonqualified deferred compensation plan is an agreement between an employer and its employees to defer a portion of their salary to a future tax year.

Box 11 shows how much the employer distributed to the employee from a nonqualified deferred compensation plan. This amount is taxable.

What’s box 12 used for?

Employers enter the codes in box 12 to further itemize their employees’ earnings and provide them with additional information that may be helpful to them.

Employers can enter up to 4 different codes, from A - II, on a single W-2. They’ll issue another W-2 if they need to enter more than 4 codes. If there are more than 4 box 12 codes distributed across multiple W-2s that are otherwise identical, enter all of the box 12 codes here.

Box 12 is sometimes divided into 4 sections labeled 12a - 12d. These labels are not code names.

Learn what each code means.

What’s a governmental 457(b) plan?

A governmental 457(b) plan is a plan established by a state or local government that allows its employees to defer income taxation on retirement savings into future years.

Learn more about 457(b) plans.

What’s box 13 used for?

In box 13, employers will check the appropriate box if the employee during the year:

  • Was a statutory employee
  • Participated in the employer’s retirement plan
  • Received sick pay under the employer’s third-party insurance policy

Statutory employee: A statutory employee is someone whose earnings are subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes, but not subject to federal income tax withholding. They are independent contractors under the common-law rules but are treated by statute as employees.

What’s box 14 used for?

Employers may use box 14 to report anything that doesn’t belong somewhere else on a Form W-2. For example, box 14 may list state disability insurance taxes withheld, union dues, health insurance premiums deducted, nontaxable income, and educational assistance payments.

How do I know if codes in box 14 relate to union dues?

Employers may list your union dues on your Form W-2 in box 14. Each employer can choose the code(s) they use for this information.

If you’re not sure if the codes in box 14 are for dues you paid for union membership, ask your employer.

What if the total listed includes funds for things other than membership dues?

Only enter the part of the amount that represents what you paid for union membership.

Don’t enter any part of the total that was for:

  • Payment of sick, accident, or death benefits
  • Pension funds (even if the union required you to contribute)
  • Certain lobbying and political activities

What if there are multiple entries in a box?

Sometimes a Form W-2 may have more than one entry in boxes 15-20.

If a box has multiple entries, you’ll need to file your federal taxes using another tool. Find other ways to file your federal taxes.

What if my Form W-2 doesn’t have information for boxes 15-20?

Sometimes an employer will send multiple copies of a Form W-2 and state income information may only appear on one copy.

If you received multiple copies of a Form W-2 from an employer, check all of the forms for state income information.

If you think you need a corrected Form W-2, contact the employer.
